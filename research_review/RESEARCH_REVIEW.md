## 1. The narrative currently being argued

**Scope correction concerning the binning notebook:** the author subsequently clarified that `binning_bias_fig.ipynb` is not part of the intended final paper. Findings specific to that notebook—bin-center snapping, its synthetic data-generation loop, and its ground-truth helper—should therefore be treated as legacy-code observations, not established defects in the final paper's simulations. The PDF/source inspected still contain the rotated-ridge validation and binning/noise-floor statements (Fig. 3, Methods §4.2 and Supplementary Fig. S4), but their generating code has not been verified. C6 and simulation-specific recommendations apply only if those claims remain in the final version. The algebraic interpretation and noise-attenuation findings concern the estimator itself and do not depend on the unused notebook.

The paper’s argument has six steps:

1. Marginal tuning can conceal interactions or inherit tuning from correlated sampling.
2. A cross-fold correlation method estimates the contribution of each variable without restrictive interaction assumptions or noise bias.
3. Visual-network units that pass conventional spatial-cell criteria nevertheless have entangled location–head-direction responses.
4. Biological place and head-direction cells show a contrasting selectivity/invariance dissociation.
5. This dissociation identifies a deficiency in visual models of spatial cognition.
6. Applying the same method to head and movement direction constrains the source of the velocity signal used for path integration.

Step 1 is sound motivation with substantial precedent. Step 2 is not valid as stated. Step 3 has suggestive visual evidence, but important protocol and estimator qualifications. Step 4 is plausible in the selected recordings, with stronger evidence for stable field shape than for response invariance. Step 5 requires careful restriction to the tested representations and level of explanation. Step 6 supports a narrower conditional-coding question and currently distracts from the model-comparison story.

The missing logical bridges are: **shape similarity → response invariance; low decoder performance → little available information; individual-unit entanglement → population failure; a model instance → an entire class of mechanisms.** None is automatic.

## 3. What the estimator actually measures

For joint response h(x,y), the implemented statistic is

\[
D_x=1-\operatorname{mean}_{i\ne j}\operatorname{corr}\{\hat h_0(x_i,\cdot),\hat h_1(x_j,\cdot)\}.
\]

The code returns the off-diagonal mean and diagonal reliability; the notebook constructs one minus the former. It does not calculate a residual sum of squares, an explained-variance ratio, or an orthogonal variance decomposition. See [implementation](../src/explained_variance.py) and the [independent mathematical/code audit](source_notes/method_audit.md).

Three simple cases settle the interpretation without any literature-dependent judgment:

| True response | What varies | Noise-free Pearson result |
|---|---|---|
| h(x,y)=u(x)+v(y), both nonconstant | Both inputs change firing; an additive code | D_x=D_y=0, because offsets disappear |
| h(x,y)=a(x)b(y), both positive and nonconstant | Both inputs change firing; multiplicative gain modulation | D_x=D_y=0, because positive scaling disappears |
| h(x,y)=u(x) | One-variable selectivity with perfect y invariance | x-conditioned slices are constant over y, so D_x is undefined |

These are not rare edge cases for this manuscript: gain-modulated place fields are part of its biological explanation. A large residual HD score cannot be attributed directly to gain that the statistic removes. With Poisson sampling, gain can indirectly alter reliability, which makes this attribution even less interpretable without calibration.

Under a simplified independent additive-noise model, observed correlations satisfy approximately

\[
r_{ij}^{obs}\approx r_{ij}^{signal}\sqrt{q_i^{(0)}q_j^{(1)}},
\]

where q is the fraction of slice variance due to signal. Independent folds do not remove the attenuation factors. If all true slices match but reliabilities differ, dividing the mean off-diagonal correlation by the mean diagonal does not generally yield one. Poorly estimated tuning can therefore look like strong dependence on both variables. This is consistent with the broader noise-correction literature, rather than a peculiarity of this implementation. [Pospisil and Bair, 2021, signal correlation](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2775-20.2021).

The joint response must also be defined relative to a distribution. An observational marginal integrates h(x,y) against p(y|x). A balanced stimulus map might instead use a fixed q(y). These answer different questions. When x and y are sampled only along a diagonal, many different joint functions fit the observations exactly; interpolation cannot identify their off-diagonal behavior.

### A repair that preserves the useful intuition

Separate three endpoints rather than make one scalar carry the whole argument:

1. **Response dependence:** how much does changing the nuisance alter the response, including gains and offsets?
2. **Conditional tuning shape:** do preferred locations/directions and field profiles persist, allowing explicitly defined gain and baseline changes?
3. **Population transfer:** does a decoder trained under some nuisance conditions work under held-out conditions without changing its weights?

For a fully observed balanced map, ordinary functional ANOVA provides an interpretable response decomposition: h=μ+a(x)+b(y)+i(x,y), with centered components orthogonal under a specified product measure. The component variances then add to total signal variance. A total effect of x includes a and the interaction and need not add with the total effect of y to one. Correlated observational inputs require a stated reference measure or a different allocation convention. This is established methodology; adopting it would be a repair and comparison, not automatically a novel contribution.

A possible estimator uses independently estimated maps and fixed linear component projections P_k: take the cross-product ⟨P_k ĥ_0, P_k ĥ_1⟩ under the chosen weights. With unbiased map estimates, fixed support/projections and independent zero-mean errors across folds, its expectation equals the corresponding squared signal component. Its ratio to an estimated total is not exactly unbiased, and smoothing, missing cells and data-dependent choices need separate treatment. Negative finite-sample estimates are possible and should be handled through uncertainty, not silent clipping. This is a candidate to validate against established covariance/distance approaches, not a ready-made guarantee.

For shape stability, compare a cross-validated hierarchy: a shared tuning curve; shared shape with nuisance-dependent gain; shared shape with gain and baseline; then a flexible interaction model. Measure held-out predictive improvement and field displacement. A gain-plus-baseline model is more permissive than a multiplicatively separable map, so these terms should not be used interchangeably. Pearson similarity remains a useful descriptive companion when the slices have measurable signal.

## 4. Problems requiring correction before rewriting conclusions

**The GLM example has a ground-truth problem.** The exported notebook computes actual ground-truth slices and then replaces them with their mean levels under the comment “\"True\" displayed as flat lines.” Those arrays are returned as true_fx and true_gy. This establishes an error in that local plotting path, not the provenance of every historical figure. Independently, the stated Gaussian-ridge model has nonflat marginals under independent Gaussian sampling. The PDF caption’s flat-marginal assertion is therefore wrong under its own Methods. See [notebook](../notebooks/glm.ipynb). Regenerate the demonstration from an explicit mathematical target.

**Model folds change a biological variable.** Camera pitches are deterministic input conditions, not repeated noisy observations of exactly the same state. The local split uses two different pitch sets. Unless their effects are controlled or distributions balanced, the resulting correlation combines location/HD structure with pitch sensitivity. This can be a valuable pitch-generalization test, but cannot be called measurement-noise correction. For a deterministic model on a complete stimulus grid, first analyze the noiseless map directly at fixed pitch; treat pitch and history as additional conditions.

**The predictive model is evaluated with repeated static images.** The notebook constructs ten identical frames and reads the last timestep. This may be a legitimate stationary-input test, but it cannot alone establish failure of the temporal representation learned during navigation. Also, the loader silently leaves the model randomly initialized when the checkpoint path is absent. The checkpoint is absent from this local folder; that does not prove that the historical results used an untrained model. It does mean their provenance must be recovered. See [evaluation](../notebooks/model_neurons.ipynb) and [loader](../src/ann/get_activations.py).

**Spatial preprocessing needs a geometry audit.** The model notebook smooths a flattened location index as one spatial axis. Flattened-index neighbors do not implement an isotropic neighborhood on the two-dimensional arena, and row boundaries can connect inappropriate locations. Smooth X, Y and circular HD in their actual geometry before any flattening. Upsampling to equalize axis sizes creates no independent observations and should not establish the fairness of axis comparisons.

**Biological uncertainty is not recoverable from the current score presentation.** The paper attributes sizable residuals to sparse sampling without showing the required cellwise reliability, occupancy and calibrated null comparisons. Randomly dividing nearby visits also needs temporal-dependence checks. Hundreds of cells do not replace independent animals or model seeds.

## 5. Literature that changes the interpretation

The annotated [methods review](source_notes/literature_methods.md) and [spatial neuroscience/model review](source_notes/literature_spatial.md) provide the wider source inventory and comparisons. The following are the consequential connections, not just papers to add to the bibliography.

The closest older precedent is unusually important. Franzius, Sprekeler and Wiskott (2007) explicitly studied visual learning of place, HD and spatial-view cells, joint position–orientation tuning, and invariance. Their analysis used average conditional variances in position and orientation. Consequently, even replacing Pearson scores with conditional variances would not make the general approach new. A modern matched comparison with neural recordings and functional transfer could still add value. [Original article](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.0030166).

The two main contemporary comparators also need to be represented accurately. Gornet and Thomson (2024) assessed spatial distances, localization and vector navigation as well as individual units. The appropriate question is whether those representations reproduce the particular conditional tuning properties being tested here. [Original article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00863-1). Luo, Mok and Love’s revised work includes population decoding and unit-removal analyses and treats object networks as informative null models. A finding of imperfect biological single-unit fidelity would partly complement that argument. Cite the November 2025 reviewed-preprint revision with its correct status. [eLife v2](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/99047v2).

Biological invariance must be region-, task- and species-specific. Rat CA1 open-field results do not define all hippocampal neurons. Heading relative to a reference point can modulate CA1 firing, and even nominally nonconjunctive grid cells can have field-specific HD modulation. These are informative positive interaction controls, not inconveniences to exclude. [Jercog et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6536526/); [Gerlei et al., 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17500-1).

The recent literature makes population tests more, not less, necessary. A Neuron article available September 9, 2026 reports spatial and gaze codes with mixed single-neuron responses and cross-condition generalization in human hippocampus. Its human virtual-task setting differs from the present rodent data, but a broad claim that spatial abstraction should appear as invariant individual cells is no longer tenable. [Article and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.004).

## 6. Recommended narrative and alternative outcomes

The most compelling question is: **When does apparent spatial selectivity imply a spatial representation that transfers across changes in viewpoint and behavior?** The empirical paper should answer this for named models and neural datasets. It should not claim that invariance is newly discovered, that mixed selectivity is intrinsically defective, or that one sensory input regime cannot yield cognitive maps.

Head direction is only a nuisance relative to a specified task. Image prediction needs information about viewing direction, and successful navigation may need both position and orientation. A useful test asks whether invariant position is accessible with a practical amount of training data, not whether every image-prediction unit discards HD. Conversely, a network designed to output position alone is a positive control with privileged task information, not evidence that invariance emerged spontaneously.

A suitable working title is **“Conditional tuning and generalization distinguish spatial representations in neural networks and hippocampal circuits.”** This title is conditional on actually measuring a distinction; it should be softened if the results do not show one. A more conservative title is **“Beyond marginal spatial tuning: a comparison of conditional response structure in brains and networks.”**

The revised argument would be:

1. Conventional spatial-cell labels leave important response properties unspecified.
2. Define and calibrate response dependence, tuning-shape stability and population transfer as separate properties.
3. Compare these properties fairly across selected biological populations and model instances.
4. Test whether apparent single-cell differences predict a difference in held-out-view readout.
5. If useful, identify one controlled change in learning objective or experience that alters the relevant property.

This structure makes the paper valuable even if the original contrast changes. The main possible outcomes imply different stories:

| Outcome of validated analyses | Defensible paper |
|---|---|
| Shape and readout gaps persist across support matching, real model histories and model seeds | A stronger benchmark showing which specific visual representations fail a biological and functional test |
| Model single cells remain unstable but fixed population readouts transfer well | A dissociation between neuron-level similarity and computational competence; avoid claiming cognitive-map failure |
| Real histories restore predictive-model stability | A result about temporal context and evaluation protocol, potentially more informative than an architecture critique |
| A simple visual slowness/readout intervention restores stability | An account of the objective, experience or coordinate basis needed for invariant coding, with old SFA work as the baseline |
| Calibration removes the biological/model difference | Withdraw the original gap claim; a methods paper is worthwhile only if the calibration result is substantive and competitive with established methods |
| No robust new contrast, transfer insight or methodological advance survives | Archive a reproducible technical report instead of extending the project indefinitely |

My ranking is: **first, a compact validated comparison that can stand on its own as a workshop contribution; second, add a transfer experiment if the first results justify it; third, a causal learning-objective expansion if that exposes a clear explanatory gap.** A stand-alone paper announcing the current correlation score is a poor investment. The HD–MD section should usually move to a secondary validation or supplement unless its conditional-information result becomes the central discovery.

For a more ambitious paper, combining familiar tools is not enough. Aim for a result that changes an inference: conventional cell labels rank models differently from calibrated biological/transfer tests; normal temporal context reverses an apparent model failure; or a controlled objective/experience manipulation explains the relevant gap. For a workshop, a carefully documented case study and reusable evaluation can be sufficient intellectual value, subject to the venue’s actual scope. This is a scope recommendation, not a prediction of acceptance.

### Minimum workshop package

Use one clearly defined question: **Do marginally place-like units in a verified visual model preserve spatial tuning across head directions, and how does that compare with one specified neural population?** A suitable title would be “A reproducible case study of conditional spatial tuning in a visual network and CA1.” Keep the abstract limited to the tested model, dataset and response property.

The minimum evidence is: (a) a correct synthetic calibration distinguishing shape from gain and noise; (b) one verified model evaluation, using natural histories if the model is temporal; (c) one reproducible biological comparison with adequate support and uncertainty if a brain claim is retained; and (d) sensitivity to the main preprocessing and selection decisions. An ordinary pretrained CNN is the lower-dependency starting point if the predictive checkpoint cannot be recovered. Include a simple known-invariant synthetic control. One independent model instance supports a case study, not a model-family conclusion.

This package uses E0–E4 and the basic parts of E7, restricted to the chosen comparison. It does **not** require the full seed/environment sweep, a new architecture, another animal dataset, a path-integration study, or all proposed estimators. A small E5 readout is a valuable addition but is optional if the claims remain about measured single-unit structure. Three main figures—calibration, joint-map comparison, and robustness—could carry it. A fourth could show transfer.

If biological data or analysis provenance cannot be restored economically, remove the brain comparison and publish a smaller artificial-representation case study or evaluation resource. Its value would have to be a verified, useful lesson about model evaluation, not simply re-demonstrating that marginals can miss interactions. If only synthetic counterexamples survive, a concise technical note or software documentation may be the more appropriate endpoint.

Budget roughly **one to three focused weeks after the needed data/code are in hand** for a tightly scoped workshop package, with a checkpoint after the first few days. Data restoration is a separate uncertainty and can dominate that estimate. The smaller target reduces breadth and ambition; it does not reduce the need for correct estimands, honest provenance and defensible statistics.

### Details that determine whether E5 is convincing

Randomly holding out samples of already observed location×HD combinations tests interpolation, not viewpoint generalization. Hold out sectors or combinations, show a curve across angular/geographic separation, and avoid nearby-frame or trajectory leakage. Train a regularized linear readout first; include a modest nonlinear readout to determine whether a gap reflects linear accessibility rather than absence of information. For directions, use circular targets/losses. For position, report error normalized by arena size as well as native units.

Match population size and evaluate learning curves versus training samples and cell count. For biological comparisons, maintain simultaneous populations where the question concerns correlated population activity; any pseudo-population analysis needs a separate label. Match or explicitly model firing/noise rather than giving deterministic model activations an unacknowledged advantage. Set selection and hyperparameters using training data only.

Use a behavioral baseline appropriate to the question. For MD, the relevant comparison is prediction from HD, speed and recent trajectory, not just uniform 90° guessing. For neural invariance, test incremental information about the nuisance conditional on the target. For absolute-error decoding, a random draw from occupancy is not generally the best no-neural predictor: use the prediction minimizing expected loss under the training occupancy as another baseline. A neural decoder performing worse than that baseline is not evidence that the neural population lacks information.

A useful small mathematical control is a rotation of the population activity coordinates: it can change apparent unit types while preserving linear readout capacity under the corresponding weight transform. It illustrates why single-cell appearance and computational access differ. An arbitrary rotation is not itself a biological mechanism; retain physical-neuron analyses and explicitly distinguish a representational-basis argument.

### A staged investment decision

Spend the first week on E0, E1 and a small E2/E4 pilot, not new architectures. The decision is whether a verified model and biological subset have reliable, interpretable conditional tuning measurements. If not, finish the calibration before expanding.

Next, spend approximately one to three weeks on E3, E5 and the basic E7 controls. Predefine the primary comparison, practical effect size of interest, unit/session aggregation and confidence intervals before inspecting the new group contrast. Repeat model instances enough to estimate seed/environment variability; choose the final count based on pilot variance rather than treating a customary number as a power calculation.

Proceed to E6/E11 only if the measurements expose a stable scientific question. The most attractive intervention is one that discriminates two explanations—temporal context versus static visual features, or training statistics versus objective—not a large model sweep. Keep MD as a separate branch unless E8 produces an unusually clear, new result.

## 8. Proposed figure and text revision

**Figure 1:** correct examples separating marginal tuning, response dependence and conditional-shape change, including additive and multiplicative cases. **Figure 2:** estimator calibration against known targets and realistic occupancy/noise. **Figure 3:** joint fields plus gain/shape summaries for selected neural populations and verified model protocols. **Figure 4:** matched population transfer with behavioral and model controls. **Figure 5, if justified:** one controlled intervention explaining a transfer or stability gap. Keep classifier details and the HD–MD application supplementary unless their results change the central question.

The introduction should begin with what conventional spatial labels leave unspecified, then define the particular missing test. Give the older invariance literature and comparator papers their actual contributions. Explain why current data/models permit a more informative comparison now. Avoid relying on novelty language about “examining multiple variables together.”

In Results, replace “equal variance contributions” with the specific measured property, and do not infer interaction strength merely from equal axis scores. Replace “cannot accurately decode” with the actual comparison, error metric, uncertainty and baseline. In Discussion, distinguish empirical observations from candidate explanations and make the model-level scope explicit.

A possible opening claim after successful reanalysis is: “Spatially selective average responses do not specify whether the underlying code preserves tuning or readout across viewing conditions. We compare these properties in identified rodent populations and visual-network representations using calibrated joint-response analysis and held-out-condition decoding.” The results sentence must remain unwritten until those analyses are complete.

## 9. Evidence available and limitations

The folder contains the manuscript, five notebooks, estimator and ANN code, Minecraft images/panoramas, older activation arrays/unit charts, and six saved ANN score archives. It does not contain the referenced raw biological recordings, the two imported biological decoder modules, or the predictive-coder checkpoint at the loader’s expected path. Some old activation arrays are substantial and may be useful, but their mapping to current pitch-based analyses needs recovery. Missing files limit reproduction; they do not establish that the original analyses were performed incorrectly.

The literature and mathematical issues do not depend on recovering those files. The exact size and robustness of the biological/model gap do. The supplied synthetic checks examine the estimator and local code path, not the truth of every manuscript result. No expensive training, raw-data download or alteration to the original source or manuscript was undertaken.

Run the lightweight audit from the project directory with `.venv/bin/python research_review/method_diagnostics.py`. It regenerates the [diagnostic results](source_notes/method_diagnostics.json) and inspects the six existing ANN archives. It does not reproduce the manuscript figures. The additive, multiplicative and pure-axis counterexamples were additionally checked by importing the original estimator module directly.

**Decision:** there is enough here to justify a focused salvage effort with a workshop-sized stopping point. There is not enough to justify a cosmetic rewrite or an unconditional commitment to the original conclusion. First establish a calibrated, reproducible comparison of response modulation and tuning-shape preservation. Add population generalization when pursuing the broader functional story, and be prepared to report that these properties dissociate.
