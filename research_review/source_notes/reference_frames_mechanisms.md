# Reference frames as a bridge to mechanistic transformer research

Targeted primary-source review, September 14, 2026. Read alongside [Wei program review](../WEI_RESEARCH_PROGRAM.md). This is a scoped assessment, not an exhaustive mechanistic-interpretability review. Proposed findings below are hypotheses; no new model experiments were run.

**Recommendation:** the reference-frame direction is a strong bridge between the two authors' interests if it becomes **identifying the coordinates a transformer uses, explaining how it computes them, and demonstrating why those coordinates support its predictions or transfer**. Finding a better-looking tuning plot after a coordinate change is insufficient. The strongest combined question is: **Does changing the prediction objective change which relational coordinate is computed and used?**

## Six close precedents

Each source summary is fewer than 80 words. The implications and proposed experiments that follow are our analysis.

1. **Nanda, Lee & Wattenberg (2023), “Emergent Linear Representations in World Models of Self-Supervised Sequence Models,” BlackboxNLP.** [Published paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.blackboxnlp-1.2/); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.00941). OthelloGPT encodes board state more directly relative to the current player (“mine/yours”) than absolute piece color. Linear-direction interventions alter subsequent legal-move predictions. The work also examines component contributions and alternative computations. This is a particularly direct precedent for an apparently complicated representation becoming interpretable through the right semantic coordinates; it is not a physical allocentric/egocentric result.

2. **Vijayabaskaran & Cheng (2022), “Navigation task and action space drive the emergence of egocentric and allocentric spatial representations,” PLOS Computational Biology.** [Full paper](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010320). Visual deep-RL agents learn aiming and guidance with egocentric, allocentric, or combined action spaces. Task and action space affect place-like versus egocentric-vector representations, and the preferred strategy benefits generalization. This already connects task demands, coordinate choice, response structure, and function. Its action-space definition of strategy should not be silently equated with discovering an internal coordinate transformation in an unconstrained visual transformer.

3. **Whittington et al. (2020), “The Tolman–Eichenbaum Machine,” Cell.** [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S009286742031388X); [open text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7707106/). TEM separates reusable relational structure from sensory identities and binds them in conjunctive representations. The model addresses spatial and nonspatial generalization and remapping, including a test of preserved structure in recordings. It supplies an established computational reason for a representation to look conjunctive while supporting transfer. Publisher summary was accessible; the PMC full-text route intermittently required a browser check.

4. **Whittington, Warren & Behrens (2022), “Relating transformers to models and neural representations of the hippocampal formation,” ICLR.** [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.04035). A modified transformer uses recurrent action-dependent position encodings; keys/queries depend on position, while values depend on sensory observations. The paper derives a connection to TEM memory retrieval and studies learned spatial responses and generalization. These are substantial architectural constraints, not evidence that an ordinary visual transformer spontaneously implements the same factorization. It is a strong mechanistic comparator and a novelty boundary for generic “transformers have cognitive maps” claims.

5. **Høydal et al. (2019), “Object-vector coding in the medial entorhinal cortex,” Nature.** [Publisher abstract and figures](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1077-7). Recorded neurons maintain preferred distances and directions relative to objects across object positions, with generalization across objects and environments. This provides an operational example of testing a physical coordinate through environmental interventions. Object-relative position and egocentric bearing are different hypotheses: the origin and the direction axis must both be specified. Full article body was subscription-limited; the abstract and figure descriptions support this summary.

6. **Piray & Daw (2025), “Reconciling flexibility and efficiency: medial entorhinal cortex represents a compositional cognitive map,” Nature Communications.** [Open paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-62733-7). A computational model composes predictive maps from reusable contributions of objects and links this organization to planning and entorhinal response types. Object-associated representations can be transformed and recombined. This is a recent precedent connecting relative representations, invariance, biological resemblance, and computational utility. The contribution is a proposed computational account rather than circuit identification in a visual transformer or a new biological intervention study.

## Three distinctions that keep the story meaningful

**A physical reference frame specifies what real-world intervention should do to the representation.** Let position be `p`, heading be `h`, and a landmark location be `l`. Candidate variables include world position `p`, the allocentric landmark vector `l − p`, and egocentric landmark vector `R(−h)(l − p)`. Their predictions differ when the animal turns in place, the landmark moves, or an external directional anchor changes. An object-centered origin does not itself imply an egocentric direction axis. The original position × heading analysis can describe these codes, but does not identify which physical variables generate the interaction.

**A latent basis change is different.** Applying the same invertible linear transformation to every activation preserves the existence of a linear readout, even though it changes individual unit tuning. That observation cannot prove an egocentric or landmark-centered code. A learned alignment for every heading, fitted without constraints, can also absorb arbitrary interactions. Evidence for a frame requires a simple transformation that predicts held-out physical interventions and, ideally, obeys composition across interventions. Not all basis changes preserve every proposed geometry statistic; specify the metric and admissible transformations.

**Feature selectivity alone does not establish a relational coordinate.** A neuron that responds when a red landmark is on the left of the image may be a visual template detector. Test variable object appearance, physical size/depth, position, background and occlusion before claiming an object-distance or remembered spatial relation. An egocentric bearing is legitimately visual, but a claim about a persistent spatial state requires more than instantaneous visibility.

Nanda et al. supply an especially useful conceptual analogy: changing **semantic labels conditional on the current player** is more informative than merely rotating the activation axes. The spatial equivalent would be identifying the relation to the observer or a landmark that makes an interpretable causal account possible. This analogy is already known; its value here is choosing an incisive experiment.

## Why this could fit Wei and the user's interests jointly

Wei's [Gao et al. (2021) group-representation work](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.10259) makes physical displacement and transformations of a population state explicit. His [Ajabi et al. (2023) landmark experiments](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-05813-2) address how a neural direction estimate changes with cues and history. His [Cueva et al. (2020) learned HD circuits](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.10189) connect mixed selectivity to a computational role. These are close precedents for asking what transformation a learned circuit implements, rather than ranking neurons by purity. This is an inference of thematic fit, not a claim about Wei's present priorities.

The user's contribution could be substantive: identify a representation, trace the components that form or update it, and intervene to test its native use. Spatial simulation offers ground-truth relational variables and exact counterfactual scenes, advantages for mechanistic work. The paper need not add language models to make that contribution relevant to transformer representations. Claiming generality beyond the studied spatial model would require another task or family of models; that can remain a later project.

## A staged experiment with a credible stopping point

### Stage 1: identify candidate coordinates on a frozen model

Use genuine trajectories and extract activations at several layers/tokens. Fit equally regularized encoding/readout models for a small prespecified family: world position, allocentric landmark vector, egocentric landmark vector, distance/bearing to boundaries, and gain/interaction alternatives. Choose candidate landmarks from scene metadata, or learn them on training data only. Compare held-out predictive likelihood or error, not just best training fit. Match support and complexity; an unconstrained nonlinear coordinate search will nearly always look better.

The existing activations may support initial fits, but fixed-scene observational data cannot establish landmark anchoring: a vector to a fixed landmark can be a reparameterization of position. Treat this as screening for a testable hypothesis. Hold out trajectories and portions of the coordinate domain, not neighboring frames.

### Stage 2: intervene in the environment to distinguish frames

Render a factorial set with two or more observer headings at matched positions, plus displaced landmarks, altered landmark appearance, and varied natural incoming histories. Derive each coordinate hypothesis's predictions before testing. Reserve novel landmark positions and heading combinations for evaluation. Use local manipulations and cue conflicts where possible, because rotating the entire observer and environment together may leave its egocentric images identical: no purely visual model can distinguish an unobservable absolute north.

Test whether a **shared low-complexity transformation** predicts representation changes at held-out positions, cues and histories. Check inverse/composition consistency on held-out cases. Include flexible encoding and image-feature controls. A good alternate frame should predict new responses or transfer failures; it should not merely relabel the original scatterplot. Broad scene edits can be out of the model's training distribution, so measure native prediction quality and begin with supported variations.

### Stage 3: test whether the proposed coordinate is used

First state a counterfactual in the model's native task: what next observation should change if the remembered landmark bearing or self-heading changes while the other relevant state is held fixed? Construct valid donor/recipient trajectories and simulator-grounded target outputs. If possible, use ambiguous or temporarily occluded observations so history must supply the variable. Do not silently add action inputs or navigation heads to a checkpoint that did not originally consume them.

Find a candidate low-dimensional state and the attention/MLP components that write, update, and read it. Patch it across matched examples, then test whether the **original model's output** changes toward the predicted counterfactual, with other output attributes preserved. An auxiliary probe changing its own prediction after a probe-directed edit is not enough. Interchange, ablation, and restoration/rescue should agree. Compare same-state donor patches, matched-norm random or irrelevant subspaces, reverse patches and untouched-model counterfactuals. Test at held-out scenes/locations and a fixed intervention strength selected without those outcomes.

A donor activation is real, but its hybrid with recipient activations can still be off-distribution. Therefore report the scope of the intervention, check activation statistics, and require specificity rather than treating any output damage as necessity. The strongest outcome identifies an algorithmic operation—for example, updating an observer-relative landmark relation from successive views—and predicts its failure under a targeted manipulation. Merely localizing a correlated feature is a weaker but potentially useful workshop result.

### Stage 4: change the objective only if the pilot exposes an interpretable mechanism

Hold architecture, trajectories and training budget as constant as possible while changing one target property: for example, immediate image prediction versus a multistep or explicitly view-stable target. Do not simultaneously change action inputs, data diversity and architecture and attribute the result to the objective. A pose-supervised auxiliary loss is an informative positive control, but its creation of pose-aligned coordinates is expected and is not by itself a discovery.

Ask whether the change causes a different coordinate or update operation to emerge and whether that explains a specific generalization benefit/tradeoff. Frozen-model stages are much cheaper than retraining, but their feasibility still depends on recovering the simulator and native predictor. Mechanistic tracing may be difficult if the relevant computation is broadly distributed; it is sensible to begin with one verified baseline and one candidate state.

## What would be new, and what would not

A focused new result could be **a predictive visual transformer's apparent spatial entanglement is a lawful, causally used observer-relative code; a particular objective changes its transformation mechanism and thereby its transfer profile**. It could also be a meaningful negative: information is decodable in a proposed coordinate but does not mediate the tested prediction, while another relational variable does. These are possible outcomes, not anticipated facts.

Weak outcomes include: a frame-specific probe beats another probe in the same arena; more place-like cells appear under location supervision; arbitrary learned rotations align representations; and a large activation deletion damages performance. Existing papers already establish task-dependent frames, conjunctive representations supporting generalization, and transformer spatial representations. If the new coordinate neither predicts unseen transformations nor explains model behavior, this branch may not justify its own paper. A rigorous small mechanistic case study remains possible without establishing a universal theory or outperforming major navigation systems.
