## Verified works and what they establish

Each source summary below is fewer than 100 words. These distinguish published results from our proposed extensions.

**Cueva & Wei (2018), “Emergence of grid-like representations by training recurrent neural networks to perform spatial localization.” ICLR 2018 conference paper.** [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.07770); [conference PDF record](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=B17JTOe0-). Velocity-driven RNNs trained for localization develop spatial response patterns, with noise, activity regularization, and movement statistics materially affecting emergence. Boundary interactions permit error correction without explicit visual input. This establishes examples of learned spatial coding under particular tasks and constraints, not a theorem that localization inevitably yields biological cell types. I read the full HTML, including regularization and discussion sections.

**Cueva, Wang, Chin & Wei (2020), “Emergence of functional and structural properties of the head direction system by optimization of recurrent neural networks.” ICLR 2020 conference paper.** [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.10189); [conference PDF record](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=HklSeREtPB). Angular integration training produces Compass and conjunctive HD×angular-velocity Shifter units. Joint tuning, connectivity, and connection lesions support their respective roles in maintaining and updating heading. Altering the angular-velocity distribution changes the balance of these unit types. The study also reports velocity-dependent tuning shifts and gains. These are computational model results and comparisons with biological observations, not proof that all biological HD circuits use the learned mechanism. Full HTML inspected.

**Gao, Xie, Wei, Zhu & Wu (2021), “On Path Integration of Grid Cells: Group Representation and Isotropic Scaling.” NeurIPS 2021 conference paper; preprint first posted 2020.** [Published record](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2021/hash/f076073b2082f8741a9cd07b789c77a0-Abstract.html); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.10259). The work studies population-vector transformations for self-motion, conditions for composition of motions, and locally isotropic scaling between physical and neural displacement. It links particular linear transformations and geometric assumptions to hexagonal response patterns and demonstrates learned path integration. Its relevant evaluation level is population geometry and dynamics, beyond cell appearance. The conditions are properties of the proposed representation framework, not universal empirical laws of every spatially tuned population.

**Ajabi, Keinath, Wei & Brandon (2023), “Population dynamics of head-direction neurons during drift and reorientation.” Nature 615, 892–899.** [Open-access full article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-05813-2). Thalamic HD population recordings with visual-landmark manipulations reveal a gain dimension in addition to represented angle. Gain covaries with reorientation and drift dynamics, and carries effects of landmark history. A computational model addresses adaptation to cues. This directly argues against treating all non-angular modulation as irrelevant contamination. It concerns manipulated landmarks and ADN population dynamics, not the present manuscript's PoSub position dependence or CA1 heading dependence. Main article and methods inspected.

**von Ebers & Wei (2024), “Cognitive maps from predictive vision.” Nature Machine Intelligence 6, 850–851, News & Views.** [Publisher page](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00885-9). This is commentary by the same two manuscript authors, not an independent experiment. Its accessible summary describes Gornet & Thomson's predictive self-attention network as generating an environmental map in latent space. The public page provides the reference list; full body access was subscription-limited, and the PDF route returned the same preview. I therefore cannot claim to have reviewed its detailed qualifications or discussion. It establishes a direct thematic connection but does not establish an author commitment to all claims of the commented-on paper.

**Xu, Gao, Zhang, Wei & Wu (2025), “On Conformal Isometry of Grid Cells: Learning Distance-Preserving Position Embedding.” ICLR 2025, oral presentation.** [Current full-text preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16865); [author publication page verifying final title and venue](https://dehongxu.github.io/). Earlier versions use “An Investigation of Conformal Isometry Hypothesis for Grid Cells.” The work relates grid patterns to learning a position embedding that locally preserves physical distance, examining recurrent models and giving theoretical arguments. This extends the population-geometric program. Avoid citing the old title as a separate 2024 finding or counting successive preprints as independent evidence.

## Main implications for this revision

### 1. A claim about functional organization fits better than biological purity

My inference from the 2020 and 2023 papers is that the productive question is: **which contextual modulations preserve, update, or destabilize a spatial representation?** A representation can preserve its preferred coordinate and still have useful contextual gain. A more conjunctive population may perform the update rather than maintain the estimate. Neither fact makes the present brain–model comparison uninteresting; both make a one-dimensional purity ranking insufficient.

For this manuscript, explicitly distinguish response invariance, stable field shape allowing gain, and robust population readout. Do not claim that the Pearson estimator measures the first merely because it measures an aspect of the second. Gain should be measured separately, even if preferred-coordinate stability remains the headline biological result. In the workshop version, it is acceptable to stop at a carefully scoped descriptive contrast if calibrated comparisons survive.

### 2. The natural sequel to the authors' News & Views is a stronger validation question

A constructive framing is: **predictive vision can organize spatial information; what additional tests establish that the resulting code has the stability and transfer properties attributed to a spatial map?** The manuscript can contribute those tests without pretending the previous papers only showed pretty individual cells. A latent map, a place-like unit, and a robust downstream location signal are distinct achievements.

It would be inappropriate to soften an empirical disagreement for social reasons. If the reproduced model fails, say precisely what failed. But failure on our evaluation cannot deny emergence of any useful map, particularly if realistic histories or alternative readouts rescue performance. The tested checkpoint and protocol must be identified. The repeated-static-frame issue is especially relevant to this narrative because a temporal predictor deserves a trajectory-conditioned evaluation.

### 3. A minimal computational addition is more coherent than a larger catalogue

My preferred expanded question is: **does conditional single-unit stability predict the ability to read location across unfamiliar viewing directions?** It makes the connection between biological response properties and useful behavior explicit, and can produce an informative result in either direction. Models could transfer despite mixed units, or the calibrated biological resemblance score could predict transfer. Either result is more compelling than asserting that high entanglement precludes a cognitive map.

This addition does not establish full path integration, planning, or mapping in unfamiliar environments. Call it cross-heading readout transfer. Treat metric geometry tests as optional extensions rather than necessities for the first revision.

### 4. Experience and circuit roles offer a plausible mechanism, but need an intervention

The 2020 result makes an intervention on training experience especially well motivated: vary turning versus translation statistics while holding the learning objective and other conditions fixed. The new result would have to concern spatial stability and transfer, not merely rediscover that input statistics affect tuning. A useful comparison would ask whether the same changes that improve population transfer also produce stable fields, or instead change which population performs which computation.

Do not conflate three different variables: head direction is angle, angular velocity is its rate of change, and movement direction is the angle of translational velocity. The 2020 paper does not validate the present claim that HD neurons lack the movement-direction information necessary for path integration. That claim still requires a speed-aware, temporal, incremental-information analysis if retained.

## Feasible experiment packages

| Package | Concrete work | Decision it enables | Feasibility and stop rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workshop core: conditional field stability | Fix estimator interpretation; fit shared-shape, gain/baseline, and flexible interaction models with held-out evaluation; reliability and occupancy controls; session/animal uncertainty; one verified visual model and neural comparison | Is there a defensible biological/model difference in shape stability, rather than a metric artifact? | Low–medium compute, meaningful analysis effort. Existing original notebooks/checkpoint improve feasibility. If difference vanishes after calibration, report only if the reason teaches a generalizable lesson. |
| First expansion: population transfer | Train fixed regularized location decoders on one set of heading sectors and evaluate on disjoint sectors, balancing locations; compare with within-sector tests, untrained features, and a positive spatial control; relate transfer to single-unit measures | Does cell-level stability explain useful readout, or does the population overcome mixed tuning? | Medium effort, often frozen features and no retraining. Guard against empty location support and temporal leakage. A single decoder's poor performance is not absence of information. |
| Essential predictive-model check | At matched location and orientation, compare natural incoming trajectories, varied realistic histories, and repeated images; vary history length and representation layer | Was apparent instability an evaluation-protocol artifact, and when does a stable spatial signal emerge? | Medium, depending on simulator/checkpoint recovery. This is a validity requirement before criticizing that model, and potentially a standalone modest result if effects are robust. |
| Mechanistic expansion: experience | Retrain otherwise matched models with altered rotation/translation statistics, multiple seeds; use the same held-out test distribution for all; score prediction, shape stability, gain, and transfer | Can experience explain the tradeoff between view prediction and spatial abstraction? | Medium–high. Begin only if baseline comparison and history check identify an effect worth explaining. Do not let differences in test occupancy stand in for learning effects. |
| Longer expansion: cue anchoring and dynamics | Change landmark appearance or orientation in simulation; measure readout drift, recovery, field displacement, and gain during/after perturbation | Does the learned spatial state preserve useful coordinates while adapting to visual evidence? | High relative to frozen-feature analysis; stronger biological link but needs temporal state and genuine cue-conflict protocol. Existing free-foraging neural recordings cannot retroactively supply matched perturbation evidence. |
| Optional geometry bridge | Compare location-displacement geometry across heading conditions, with cross-validation and a specified normalization; test whether transformations compose consistently | Is map-like population geometry stable even when individual fields differ? | Medium–high methodological scope. Transfer is easier to interpret first. Do not require gridness or conformal isometry from a model trained for visual prediction. |

## Proposed short pitch to discuss with the coauthor

“We can turn the draft into a test of which properties of spatial representations survive changes in viewpoint. The first step separates field-shape stability from gain and sampling effects. The key addition asks whether those cell-level properties predict location readout across headings, evaluating the predictive network with genuine navigation histories. That would let us say exactly what visual learning already provides and what remains different from the recorded spatial populations. A calibrated comparison is a viable workshop endpoint; if transfer or history exposes a clear dissociation, we have a focused route to a broader paper.”

This is a suggested framing for future discussion, not a message sent to the author and not a prediction of his preferences. We should take concrete calibrated results, not a promise of a broad new method, to that discussion.
